Products that make life easier

Most objects are built for one fixed job in one fixed space. Ours adapt to the people who use them and the places they stand in.

Every project starts from a specific everyday problem and ends in a mechanism: a set of links, pivots and stops that gives one product several stable configurations, each reached with a single, simple input.

Human-actuated

No motors, batteries or electronics. Gravity and the user's hand drive every change of shape.

Modular

Products are designed to come apart, travel and reassemble, so they suit spaces a fixed installation can't.

Engineering and product design

Kinematic synthesis, CAD, structural analysis and fabrication, combined with ergonomics, materials and finish.

Our design process

  1. Problem definitionA specific spatial or functional need, framed as a clear brief.
  2. Mechanism & ergonomicsKinematics and motion paths developed alongside usability.
  3. CAD & hand-held prototypeSmall-scale models to validate motion and constraints.
  4. Full-scale prototypeA working build of the chosen design.
  5. Finishing & refinementMaterial, finish and performance brought to exhibit standard.

Twoble

A two-level table: dining height or lounge height.

Lift the top and the crossed legs swing through a quarter turn on a hinge-slider. Set it down and it stands at the other height.

Final product

Batwing Display

A reconfigurable exhibit display.

Inspired by the way a bat folds its wings, the display's two wings fold and open together from a central post, each carrying four panels.

Full-size prototype

Adaptable

A storage cabinet with a fold-out desk.

A single arm turns a plank about one pivot: flush with the side panels when closed, flush with the top when open.

Hand-held prototype

Storage Ladder

A cabinet door that becomes a ladder.

The triangular steps roll out from the face of the door and lock level as it leans out, then roll back flush when it closes.

Hand-held prototype

Three-level table

One table, three heights.

Rotating the top tilts the legs on universal joints, changing the table's height. Turning one way gives floor height; turning the other way gives chair height.
